Macrophage Polarization & the NLRP3 Inflammasome

A four-module onboarding program for research associates entering an innate-immunology or oncoimmunology bench, built from objectives through modules, activities, and assessment.

AudienceIncoming lab RAs / trainees
FormatSelf-paced + bench practicum
Duration~6 hrs over 2 weeks
AuthorKrishna Prasath S K
01

Learning objectives

By the end of this program, the trainee will be able to:

  1. 1 Explain
    the molecular basis of M1/M2 macrophage polarization and NLRP3 inflammasome assembly, including their role in tumor-microenvironment signaling.
  2. 2 Perform
    RNA isolation, cDNA synthesis, and RT-qPCR to quantify polarization marker expression from macrophage culture.
  3. 3 Design
    a flow cytometry gating strategy that distinguishes M1 (CD86⁺) from M2 (CD206⁺) activation states.
  4. 4 Interpret
    ELISA cytokine profiles (IL-1β, IL-18, IL-10) to classify a phenotype shift and support it with data.
  5. 5 Document
    each workflow to GLP standard, including deviations, so results are audit-ready and reproducible.
02

Program modules

Module 1 · Foundations

Innate immunity & the NLRP3 inflammasome

60–75 min
Content outline
  • Pattern recognition receptors and inflammasome priming
  • NLRP3–ASC–caspase-1 assembly and IL-1β/IL-18 release
  • How inflammasome state biases M1/M2 polarization
Activity

Case study: annotate how PVA-nanoparticle exposure shifts NLRP3 activation in a published macrophage dataset, then predict the resulting M1/M2 balance.

Module 2 · Wet-lab skills

From sample to signal: RT-qPCR

90 min + bench time
Content outline
  • Column-based vs. phenol-chloroform RNA isolation
  • cDNA synthesis and NanoDrop QC (260/280, 260/230)
  • Primer validation and ΔΔCt quantification
Activity

Given raw Ct values for iNOS (M1) and Arg1 (M2) against a housekeeping gene, calculate fold-change and classify the culture's polarization state.

Module 3 · Phenotyping

Flow cytometry & cytokine profiling

75 min
Content outline
  • Gating strategy: viability → singlets → CD86⁺ / CD206⁺
  • DLS for nanoparticle size/uptake context
  • ELISA panel design for IL-1β, IL-18, IL-10
Activity

Build a gating tree from a sample FCS export, then cross-check the resulting phenotype call against an ELISA cytokine table for the same well.

Module 4 · Compliance

GLP documentation & reporting

45 min
Content outline
  • GLP record-keeping and traceability standards
  • Writing a deviation report that a reviewer can act on
  • Data integrity checks before sign-off
Activity

Draft a mock SOP excerpt for a CO₂-monitoring step in bioreactor scale-up, including a deviation note for an out-of-range reading.

03

Sample assessment — Module 2 quiz

Q1Which 260/280 absorbance ratio indicates acceptably pure RNA for downstream RT-qPCR?

  • 1.2–1.4
  • 1.8–2.0
  • 2.4–2.6

Q2A well shows Ct(iNOS) = 24.1, Ct(Arg1) = 29.8, Ct(housekeeping) = 22.3 (untreated control: ΔCt(iNOS) = 3.5, ΔCt(Arg1) = 4.2). What does the fold-change indicate?

ΔCt(iNOS) = 24.1 − 22.3 = 1.8 ΔΔCt = 1.8 − 3.5 = −1.7 Fold-change = 2^1.7 ≈ 3.2× increase in iNOS → M1-skewed

Q3Why is phenol-chloroform extraction sometimes preferred over column-based kits for downstream inflammasome-marker work?

  • It is faster per sample
  • It recovers higher yields from small, low-input macrophage cultures
  • It removes the need for a NanoDrop check
04

Practical assessment rubric

Scored during the Module 3 bench practicum, where the trainee runs the flow cytometry panel independently.

Criterion Developing Meets expectations Exceeds expectations
Technique accuracy Gating applied but boundaries misplaced on ≥2 populations Correct viability, singlet, and marker gates with minor drift Gating strategy justified against compensation controls, no drift
Data interpretation States % positive but doesn't link to phenotype Correctly classifies M1/M2 shift from marker + cytokine data Reconciles flow, RT-qPCR, and ELISA results into one coherent call
Documentation quality Missing lot numbers or timestamps Complete GLP record, traceable reagents and instrument settings Record anticipates reviewer questions; deviations pre-flagged
Troubleshooting Escalates without attempting a first diagnosis Identifies likely cause (e.g. compensation, RNA degradation) Diagnoses root cause and proposes a corrected protocol
